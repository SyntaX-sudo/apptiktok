1. Overview

This policy describes the information THXS CONTROL may process when you use its desktop features. Actual processing depends on the version you install and the permissions you enable.

2. Information Processed

  • TikTok profile data: If official login is enabled and authorized, the application may receive account identifiers, display name, avatar, and username where approved permissions allow.
  • Authentication data: OAuth authorization codes or access tokens may be processed to complete authorized login.
  • Livestream event data: If gift detection is enabled, the application may process gift names, counts, sender public usernames, and event timestamps.
  • Local configuration and diagnostic data: Settings, connection status, and error messages may be stored or shown on your device.

THXS CONTROL does not require you to provide your TikTok password directly to the application.

3. Purposes

Information is used to identify the authorized account, connect supported livestream features, respond to gift events, execute user-enabled desktop actions, display status, and troubleshoot errors.

4. How Data Is Stored

Depending on the implementation, settings and logs may be kept locally on your computer. OAuth tokens, if persisted, should be stored using secure operating-system credential facilities. Before publishing or deploying the app, verify that its actual token storage and logging behavior match this policy.

5. Sharing and Third Parties

Account authorization involves TikTok services. Livestream monitoring may involve unofficial third-party integration libraries. THXS CONTROL does not intend to sell personal information or share it for advertising. External services operate under their own privacy policies.

6. Retention and Deletion

Local settings and logs may remain until removed by the user. You may revoke TikTok authorization using TikTok's available controls. To ask about access, deletion, or other privacy rights, email nan.nascimento.2022@gmail.com. Requests will be handled subject to applicable law and the application's actual data storage capabilities.

7. Security

Reasonable safeguards should be used to protect tokens and local data. No method of transmission or storage is completely secure. Never publish access tokens, client secrets, or personal credentials in a public repository.
